# URACE

**Universal Recursive Autonomous Co-Founder Engine**

> **It drives the boat. You pick the destination.**

URACE is an **executor-agnostic persistent autonomous Product-evolution control plane**.

It turns bounded and replaceable AI/executor sessions into a **continuous autonomous Product lifecycle** governed by authoritative Intent, bounded Authority, Evidence, independent validation, durable state, and recovery.

At its core, URACE is also an **open and editable idea expressed as an open-source blueprint** through `URACE.md`. This is deliberate: the lifecycle architecture can be inspected, adapted, extended, and evolved without being architecturally dependent on any particular Product, AI, executor, or implementation.

```text
                 AUTHORITATIVE SOURCE
                         │
                         ▼
                  INTENT + AUTHORITY
                         │
                         ▼
                    ┌─────────┐
Evidence / Reality ─►  URACE  │
                    └────┬────┘
                         │
                  owns navigation
                         │
              ┌──────────┴──────────┐
              ▼                     ▼
           PRODUCT              URACE ITSELF
                                 if justified
                                 + authorized
```

You retain whatever destination-setting Authority you choose.

**URACE drives what you delegate.**

---

## Why URACE?

AI systems can research, reason, plan, code, test, analyze, and execute.

But individual executions are bounded.

```text
USER
 │
 ▼
choose task
 │
 ▼
AI / AGENT
 │
 ▼
result
 │
 ▼
USER
 │
 └── choose what happens next ──↺
```

Sessions end. Context disappears. Models change. Executors fail or are replaced.

Without a persistent lifecycle owner, the user remains the actual orchestrator of Product evolution.

URACE changes that boundary:

```text
discover
   │
   ▼
decide
   │
   ▼
prioritize
   │
   ▼
plan
   │
   ▼
select capability
   │
   ▼
execute
   │
   ▼
observe
   │
   ▼
validate
   │
   ▼
checkpoint
   │
   ▼
reassess ──────────────↺
```

The user does not have to continuously provide the next task, route, executor, or ordinary course correction.

**Models improve. Agents change. Orchestrators come and go. The autonomous Product lifecycle remains.**

## Minimum Intelligent Setup

URACE does not require a multi-agent stack.

At its simplest:

```text
Idea / Product Intent
        +
  1 capable AI
        │
        ▼
     URACE.md
        │
     bootstrap
        │
        ▼
      URACE
        │
        ▼
      Product
```

> **One idea + one capable AI is enough to start.**

The **idea** is the initial Product seed or authoritative Intent. It may instead be an existing Product, problem, mandate, desired outcome, or other sufficiently defined starting point.

The **capable AI** fulfills the initial Executor role for the Operations required by the applicable bootstrap and lifecycle.

This is the minimum, not the ceiling.

A richer setup can introduce an optional Orchestrator and multiple complementary Executors without changing who owns the Product lifecycle.

## Core Model

URACE separates responsibilities that are easy to conflate:

```text
INTENT
  └──► destination

AUTHORITY
  └──► legitimate autonomous decision space

EVIDENCE
  └──► reality

URACE
  └──► navigation

EXECUTORS
  └──► capabilities

VALIDATION
  └──► acceptance
```

These distinctions are fundamental.

Evidence can justify changing an authorized route.

Evidence can justify changing a destination whose evolution has been delegated.

Evidence does **not** independently create Authority to change a retained destination.

Executor capability does **not** create Authority.

Executor output does **not** automatically become accepted Product state.

> **URACE owns persistent autonomous Product navigation. Replaceable Executors supply whatever intelligence or execution is required to advance it.**

## Core Design Choices

URACE deliberately separates:

```text
Persistent lifecycle ownership
        │
        └──► URACE, not executor sessions

Retained Authority
        │
        └──► authoritative source decides

Delegated Authority
        │
        └──► URACE decides

Evidence
        │
        └──► constrains beliefs about reality,
             not Authority

Execution
        │
        └──► does not imply acceptance

Runtime activity
        │
        └──► does not define lifecycle persistence

Self-evolution
        │
        └──► does not imply self-authorization

Blueprint
        │
        └──► open/editable independently of
             Product-specific intelligence
```

The governing principle is:

> **Maximum justified autonomy inside applicable Authority; zero intentional autonomy outside it.**

URACE is intended to preserve these lifecycle invariants without prescribing one implementation, model, runtime, provider, repository structure, or orchestration stack.

## You Decide What You Retain

Autonomy does not require unlimited Authority.

The authoritative source determines what remains retained and what is delegated.

### Retained Destination

For example:

```text
YOU
 │
 ▼
"Build Product X"
 │
 │ destination retained
 ▼
URACE
 │
 ├── discover Objectives
 ├── prioritize
 ├── choose strategy
 ├── choose architecture
 ├── choose experiments
 ├── choose implementation
 ├── choose Executors
 └── correct course
          │
          ▼
      PRODUCT X
```

URACE may autonomously change the route.

It does not silently replace retained destination X with Y.

### Delegated Product Direction

The authoritative source may delegate more:

```text
YOU
 │
 ▼
"Solve problem X.

Choose and evolve the Product
realization when justified."
 │
 ▼
URACE
 │
 ▼
Evidence + Assessment
 │
 ▼
autonomously choose
and evolve direction
```

Here the higher-order Intent remains authoritative while subordinate Product direction has been delegated.

```text
RETAINED DECISION  ──► authoritative source decides

DELEGATED DECISION ──► URACE decides
```

Once a decision is validly delegated, URACE should not turn that delegation into a repeated approval loop.

## Evidence-Driven Evolution

URACE uses Evidence to constrain what it may defensibly conclude about reality.

```text
Product / Users / Market / Environment
                  │
                  ▼
               EVIDENCE
                  │
                  ▼
                ASSESS
                  │
                  ▼
          justified change
                  │
                  ▼
          preserve / evolve
```

Evidence may come from Product behavior, users, customers, prospects, research, analytics, transactions, adoption, retention, experiments, external systems, deterministic measurement, executor research, authoritative input, or other relevant sources.

But:

```text
Evidence ≠ Intent
Evidence ≠ Authority
Evidence ≠ automatic truth

Executor claim ≠ Evidence by default

Quantity ≠ quality
```

Material uncertainty and conflicting Evidence should be preserved rather than converted into artificial certainty.

## Recursive Self-Evolution

URACE itself can participate in the lifecycle it governs.

This is the **recursive** part of URACE.

A user does not necessarily have to notice a limitation in URACE and explicitly request its improvement.

URACE may discover that evolving its own implementation is a justified way to better advance governing Intent.

```text
                   GOVERNING INTENT
                         │
                         ▼
                       URACE
                         │
                      assess
                         │
             ┌───────────┴───────────┐
             ▼                       ▼
        PRODUCT GAP               URACE GAP
             │                       │
             ▼                       ▼
     Product Objective      Self-Evolution Objective
             │                       │
             └───────────┬───────────┘
                         ▼
                     PRIORITIZE
                         │
                         ▼
                        PLAN
                         │
                         ▼
                      EXECUTE
                         │
                         ▼
                      VALIDATE
                         │
                         ▼
                     CHECKPOINT
                         │
                         ▼
                      REASSESS
                         │
                         └────────────↺
```

Self-evolution competes with Product work through the normal Objective and Priority lifecycle.

Where existing Authority permits, URACE may autonomously discover the need for its own evolution, promote it into an Objective, prioritize it, plan it, execute it through replaceable capabilities, validate it, checkpoint it, and continue operating.

### Self-Evolution Is Not Self-Authorization

Autonomous initiation and Authority remain separate:

```text
URACE discovers justified self-evolution
                 │
                 ▼
          resolve Authority
            ┌────┴────┐
            ▼         ▼
      AUTHORIZED   NOT AUTHORIZED
            │         │
            ▼         ▼
          evolve    preserve boundary
```

```text
Self-discovery       ✓
Self-prioritization  ✓
Self-planning        ✓
Self-evolution       ✓ where authorized
Self-validation      ✓
Self-checkpointing   ✓

Self-authorization   ✗
```

URACE may not use self-evolution to create Authority, broaden delegation, override retained Intent, remove protected constraints, or bypass validation.

> **URACE may autonomously initiate its own evolution. It may not autonomously expand the Authority under which that evolution occurs.**

## Execution Roles

URACE depends on **roles and capabilities**, not particular products.

### Executor

An **Executor** is a replaceable system through which URACE delegates a bounded Operation requiring intelligence and/or execution.

An Executor may be an AI, agent, tool, API, script, deterministic system, orchestrator-mediated capability, human-approved mechanism, or future system.

### Capable Executor

An Executor is **capable for a particular Operation** when it can accept the required bounded context, perform that Operation under applicable constraints, and return enough observable result information for URACE to evaluate and validate the outcome.

Capability is contextual, not a permanent label attached to a particular product.

A **capable AI** is therefore an AI able to fulfill the Executor role for the Operations required by the applicable lifecycle.

### Orchestrator

An **Orchestrator** is an optional replaceable execution-layer system that coordinates one or more Executors.

It may provide routing, sessions, continuation, status/results, failure handling, cancellation, context coordination, or related execution services.

### Capable Orchestrator

An Orchestrator is **capable for a deployment** when it provides enough execution control and observability for URACE to delegate and supervise the required bounded Operations through it while preserving URACE's lifecycle ownership and Executor replaceability.

```text
                 URACE
                   │
          lifecycle ownership
                   │
                   ▼
             ORCHESTRATOR
               optional
                   │
       ┌───────────┼───────────┐
       ▼           ▼           ▼
   EXECUTOR    EXECUTOR    EXECUTOR
```

> **Executors provide capabilities. Orchestrators coordinate execution. URACE owns the Product lifecycle.**

### Minimum

One capable Executor is sufficient for a lifecycle whose required Operations that Executor can perform.

A capable AI may fulfill that role:

```text

```

Examples may include Codex, Claude Code, Gemini CLI, or another suitable system.

These are examples, not architectural dependencies.

### Suggested Richer Setup

Where multiple complementary capabilities are useful:

```text

                      │
                      ▼
             CAPABLE ORCHESTRATOR
              e.g. OpenHands
                      │
          ┌───────────┼───────────┐
          ▼           ▼           ▼
        Codex     Claude Code   Gemini CLI
```

Other capable models, agents, tools, APIs, deterministic systems, local models, or future Executors may be substituted.

```text
OpenHands        ≠ requirement
Three AIs        ≠ requirement
These AIs        ≠ requirement
Multi-AI         ≠ requirement
```

The topology is richer, not more authoritative.

URACE remains the lifecycle owner above both Orchestrator and Executors.

## Replaceable Intelligence

URACE should not require the conversational history of a particular Executor to preserve Product continuity.

```text
URACE
 │
 ▼
Executor A
 │
 ▼
bounded result
 │
 ▼
observe + validate
 │
 ▼
checkpoint
 │
 ▼
Executor A disappears
 │
 ▼
Executor B
 │
 ▼
continue
```

The durable lifecycle belongs to URACE.

A replacement Executor receives the bounded context required for its work rather than requiring the previous Executor's complete conversation.

Replaceability does not imply equivalence in capability, privacy, security, cost, reliability, specialization, context capacity, or data handling.

## Universal by Design

URACE is designed to remain:

**AI · Executor · Orchestrator · programming-language · file-format · Product-type · platform · repository · version-control · build-system · runtime · persistence · cloud-provider agnostic.**

Its generic lifecycle is:

> **Authoritative Source → Intent + Authority → Assessment → Objective → Priority → Plan → Operation → Executor → Observation → Validation → Checkpoint → Reassessment**

Software is only one possible Product form.

The implementation can be environment-specific while the lifecycle semantics remain independent of that implementation.

# Bootstrap

URACE currently uses **`URACE.md` as a prompt-as-bootstrap-file**.

```text
URACE.md
   │
   │ specification
   ▼
BOOTSTRAP CAPABILITY
   │
   │ creates
   ▼
RUNNING URACE
```

`URACE.md` is **not** the running system.

It defines the architecture, invariants, lifecycle semantics, implementation constraints, behavioral tests, and bootstrap requirements.

A capable Executor consumes that specification, inspects the actual Product environment, and creates an environment-appropriate persistent implementation.

---

## Bootstrap Technique

### 1. Provide the Starting Context

Provide `URACE.md` together with the relevant starting context:

```text
TARGET PRODUCT / ENVIRONMENT
           │
           ├── idea / Product Intent
           ├── Product artifacts
           ├── existing state
           ├── constraints
           ├── available capabilities
           └── URACE.md
```

The target does not have to be software.

Git, a particular programming language, or a particular framework is not an architectural requirement.

### 2. Choose a Bootstrap Capability

The minimum intelligent setup can use one capable AI Executor.

A richer setup can use an Orchestrator coordinating multiple complementary Executors.

For example:

```text
                    URACE.md
                       │
                       ▼
                    OpenHands
                       │
           ┌───────────┼───────────┐
           ▼           ▼           ▼
         Codex     Claude Code   Gemini CLI
                       │
                       ▼
                BOOTSTRAP URACE
```

This is an example topology, not a dependency.

### 3. Bootstrap from the Specification

A suitable bootstrap instruction is:

```text
Read URACE.md completely.

Treat it as the authoritative bootstrap specification.

Inspect the current Product, repository, environment, available
capabilities, constraints, existing state, and relevant artifacts.

Bootstrap the smallest complete implementation satisfying URACE.md.

Do not merely summarize, simulate, or describe URACE.

Implement the persistent lifecycle system itself.

Preserve the architectural boundary:
URACE owns persistent autonomous Product navigation;
replaceable executors provide bounded intelligence and execution.

Establish the required durable state, Authority and Intent semantics,
Evidence and Trigger handling, Objective discovery, prioritization,
planning, executor boundary, validation, checkpointing, recovery,
effect integrity, dormancy, wakeability, and autonomous lifecycle.

Preserve the recursive model:
URACE must be capable of autonomously discovering when evolution of its
own implementation is justified by governing Intent and current
conditions. Where applicable Authority permits, it may promote that
self-evolution into an Objective, prioritize it against other work,
plan it, execute it through replaceable capabilities, validate it,
checkpoint it, and continue operating without requiring a new user
request.

Self-evolution must never be treated as permission for URACE to create
Authority, broaden delegation, override retained Intent, remove
protected constraints, or bypass validation.

Adapt the implementation to this environment rather than assuming a
particular language, framework, repository structure, database,
orchestration system, or executor.

When multiple executors are available, preserve them as replaceable
capabilities rather than embedding lifecycle ownership in any one
executor or orchestrator.

Run the applicable behavioral tests and bootstrap demonstrations defined
by URACE.md.

Repair failures until the implementation satisfies the applicable
invariants and success criteria.

Then report:
1. what was implemented;
2. how URACE is invoked;
3. where durable state lives;
4. which executors/capabilities are available;
5. which Authority and Intent were established;
6. which validation was completed;
7. any unresolved constraints or retained decisions;
8. how persistent autonomous operation is started.
```

The technique is intentionally implementation-agnostic:

```text
URACE.md
   │
   ▼
UNDERSTAND ENVIRONMENT
   │
   ▼
BUILD SMALLEST VALID IMPLEMENTATION
   │
   ▼
TEST INVARIANTS
   │
   ▼
REPAIR FAILURES
   │
   ▼
VALIDATED RUNNING URACE
```

## Validate the Bootstrap

Generating files is not sufficient.

The implementation should demonstrate the applicable URACE invariants, including:

- single capable Executor operation and Executor replacement;
- durable lifecycle continuity across fresh sessions and interruptions;
- authoritative Intent, retained/delegated Authority, and destination preservation;
- autonomous Objective discovery, prioritization, planning, replanning, and Executor selection;
- Evidence and Trigger handling, course correction, validation, checkpointing, recovery, and effect reconciliation;
- dormancy, wakeability, and multi-cycle Product evolution;
- autonomous discovery of justified self-evolution, authorized self-evolution, and absence of self-authorization;
- multi-executor and Orchestrator replacement behavior where those layers are present.

A system that merely repeatedly calls an AI is not necessarily URACE.

A system that requires the user to continually choose Objectives, Plans, routes, Executors, ordinary course corrections, or necessary already-authorized self-improvements has not implemented URACE's intended autonomous boundary.

After bootstrap, use the generated persistent implementation rather than treating `URACE.md` as the operational loop.

Conceptually:

```text

# Assess, discover, prioritize, and plan

urace --autonomous
```

The actual interface may be a CLI, API, service, daemon, worker, container, agent interface, scheduled runtime, serverless runtime, or another suitable mechanism.

The interface is replaceable.

The lifecycle semantics are not.

A narrow delegation might be:

```text
Build Product X.

Preserve properties A and B.

You may autonomously change architecture, implementation,
Objectives, Plans, experiments, tools, and Executors.
```


```text

Only locally hosted Executors may receive confidential Evidence.
```

URACE resolves those boundaries before dependent commitment.

## Autonomous Executor Selection

When multiple capabilities are available, URACE may select among them according to the required Operation and applicable Authority, capability, constraints, reliability, cost, latency, context, specialization, availability, privacy, and policy.

No particular Executor needs a permanent universal role, and every Objective does not need every available Executor.

## Intervene Without Becoming the Pilot

The authoritative source can still:

- change a retained destination;
- grant, narrow, or revoke delegation;
- introduce or modify HARD constraints;
- restrict Executors or disclosure;
- pause or resume operation;
- explicitly terminate the lifecycle.

URACE then reassesses and continues within the resulting boundary.

Intervention does not require returning ordinary navigation to the authoritative source.

# Integrity and Recovery

## Validation Before Acceptance

Executor output is not automatically accepted Product state.

```text
EXECUTE
   │
   ▼
OBSERVE
   │
   ▼
VALIDATE
  ┌┴─────────┐
  ▼          ▼
FAIL        PASS
 │            │
 ▼            ▼
repair    CHECKPOINT
```

Validation may consider correctness, observed effect, governing Intent, Authority, Evidence quality, constraints, unintended effects, and material uncertainty.

> **“The Executor said it was done” is not itself a checkpoint.**

## Effect Integrity

URACE keeps these states distinct:

> **Intent → attempt → external effect → observation → validation → acceptance**

If an external effect is uncertain, URACE preserves that uncertainty and reconciles where practical rather than silently assuming success, failure, or retry safety.

## Recovery

```text
CRASH / STOP / RESTART
         │
         ▼
LOAD DURABLE STATE
         │
         ▼
RESTORE INTENT + AUTHORITY
         │
         ▼
RECONCILE UNCERTAIN EFFECTS
         │
         ▼
RESTORE OBJECTIVE / PLAN
         │
         ▼
REASSESS REALITY
         │
         ▼
CONTINUE
```

The lifecycle should not depend on reconstructing Product state from previous AI chat history.

# Executor Data and Privacy

URACE being open and executor-agnostic does **not** determine what an external AI Executor does with information sent to it.

An external service may transmit, retain, log, review, or otherwise process submitted information according to its provider, service, configuration, contractual terms, retention policy, deployment mode, organizational controls, and current policies.

Therefore:

```text
PROPRIETARY PRODUCT STATE
           │
           ▼
         URACE
           │
           ▼
"May this Executor receive it?"
           │
       ┌───┴───┐
       ▼       ▼
      YES      NO
       │       │
       ▼       ▼
 minimize   choose another
 context    capability
```

Capability does not create disclosure Authority.

Before providing proprietary, confidential, personal, regulated, security-sensitive, or otherwise restricted information to an Executor, verify the data practices and configuration applicable to the actual service being used.

Deployments may instead use appropriate API or enterprise configurations, local or self-hosted models, isolated environments, deterministic tools, data minimization, or other suitable mechanisms.

URACE does not guarantee the independent confidentiality, retention, security, or data practices of third-party Executors.

# URACE

**Universal Recursive Autonomous Co-Founder Engine**

> **It drives the boat. You pick the destination.**

URACE is an **executor-agnostic persistent autonomous Product-evolution control plane**.

It preserves and governs a continuous Product lifecycle across bounded, replaceable, and potentially stateless intelligence and execution systems.

**You choose the destination you want to retain. URACE drives everything you delegate beneath it.** Within that delegated Authority, URACE independently discovers what should happen next, prioritizes, plans, selects capabilities, executes, learns from Evidence, validates results, checkpoints accepted progress, recovers across interruptions, becomes dormant when no justified action exists, reactivates when meaningful change occurs, and continues evolving the Product.

At its core, URACE is also an **open and editable idea expressed as an open-source blueprint** through `URACE.md`. This is deliberate: the lifecycle architecture can be inspected, adapted, extended, and evolved without being architecturally dependent on any particular Product, AI, Executor, Orchestrator, or implementation.

```text
                          │

             PRODUCT             URACE ITSELF
                             if justified + authorized
```

**You choose what you retain. URACE drives what you delegate.**

Most AI-assisted Product development still behaves roughly like this:

```text

 │
 ▼
AI / Agent
 │
 ▼
result
 │
 ▼
User chooses what happens next
```

The intelligence may be powerful, but lifecycle navigation still belongs to the user or to an individual execution session.

URACE moves that responsibility into a persistent layer:

```text
discover
   │
decide
   │
prioritize
   │
plan
   │

   │
execute
   │
observe
   │
validate
   │
checkpoint
   │
reassess
   └──────────────► repeat
```

Within applicable Authority, you do not need to continuously provide the next task, route, Executor, Plan, ordinary course correction, or decision already delegated to URACE.

Models improve. Agents change. Orchestrators come and go.

**The autonomous Product lifecycle remains.**

## Core Model

URACE separates responsibilities that are often collapsed together:

```text
INTENT       → destination
AUTHORITY    → legitimate autonomous decision space
EVIDENCE     → reality
URACE        → navigation
EXECUTORS    → capabilities
VALIDATION   → acceptance
```

**Intent** describes what the Product is ultimately trying to accomplish.

**Authority** determines which decisions you retain and which URACE may make autonomously.

**Evidence** constrains what URACE may defensibly conclude about reality.

**URACE** owns persistent lifecycle navigation inside applicable Authority.

**Executors** provide bounded intelligence and/or execution.

**Validation** determines whether observed results become accepted Product state.

Evidence may justify changing a route or a delegated destination. It does not create Authority to change a destination you retained.

Executor capability does not create Authority.

Executor output does not automatically become accepted Product state.

> **URACE owns persistent autonomous Product navigation. Replaceable Executors supply whatever intelligence or execution is required to advance it.**

The governing principle is:

> **Maximum justified autonomy inside applicable Authority; zero intentional autonomy outside it.**

## Intent + Authority

Intent and Authority are designed to work together.

```text
INTENT
"What should be accomplished?"
        +
AUTHORITY
"What may URACE decide
while pursuing it?"
        │
        ▼
GOVERNING AUTONOMOUS SCOPE
        │
        ▼
URACE navigates autonomously
inside that scope
```

Intent without Authority does not establish which decisions URACE may autonomously make.

Authority without Intent does not establish what those decisions should advance.

> **Intent defines what URACE is trying to accomplish. Authority defines how much of the journey URACE owns. Together, they define the governing scope of autonomous Product evolution.**

For example:

```text
Intent:
  Build Product X.

Authority:
  Retain:
    - Product X
    - requirement A

  Delegate:
    - Objectives and prioritization
    - strategy
    - experiments
    - architecture
    - implementation
    - Plans
    - Executor selection
```

URACE may then autonomously navigate everything delegated beneath `Product X`, while preserving `Product X` and requirement A.

A broader configuration may instead be:

```text
Intent:
  Solve problem X for Y.

Authority:
  Retain:
    - problem X
    - beneficiary Y
    - constraint A

  Delegate:
    - Product realization
    - Product direction below problem X
    - Objectives and prioritization
    - strategy and experiments
    - architecture and implementation
    - Executor selection
```

In this case, URACE may autonomously determine and evolve the Product realization while preserving the higher-order destination you retained.

Conceptually:

```text
Intent + Authority
        │
        ▼
highest retained destination
        +
delegated decision space
        │
        ▼
autonomous navigation
```

This combination is established during bootstrap and remains part of the durable lifecycle state.

## You Decide What You Retain

The starting goal does not become disposable merely because URACE is autonomous.

**If you choose a destination and do not delegate Authority to change it, URACE preserves that destination.**

```text
RETAINED DECISION
        │
        ▼
     YOU decide


DELEGATED DECISION
        │
        ▼
    URACE decides
```

For example:

```text
YOU
 │
 ▼
"Build Product X"
 │
 │ retained destination
 ▼
URACE
 │
 ├── discover Objectives
 ├── prioritize
 ├── choose strategy
 ├── plan
 ├── choose architecture
 ├── run experiments
 ├── select Executors
 ├── implement
 └── correct course
 │
 ▼
PRODUCT X
```

URACE may radically change the journey without silently replacing `Product X`.

Alternatively:

```text

 │
 ▼

 │
 ▼
```

```text

     │
     ▼

     │
     ▼
```

A retained destination remains retained until valid Authority changes it.

A delegated decision belongs to URACE while that delegation remains applicable.

Stable delegation should not become a repeated approval loop.

> **Autonomy changes who navigates the delegated journey. It does not silently change the destination you chose to retain.**

## Evidence-Driven Evolution

Autonomy without reality is drift.

URACE therefore treats Evidence as a first-class lifecycle input.

Evidence may come from Product behavior, runtime observations, users, customers, prospects, analytics, transactions, adoption, activation, conversion, retention, churn, repeated use, realized outcomes, experiments, research, external systems, deterministic measurement, Executor research, or authoritative input.

```text
Product / Users / Market / Environment
                  │
                  ▼
               EVIDENCE
                  │
                  ▼
                ASSESS
                  │
                  ▼
          justified change
                  │
                  ▼
          preserve or evolve
```

But:

```text
Evidence ≠ Intent
Evidence ≠ Authority
Evidence ≠ automatic truth
Executor claim ≠ Evidence by default
Quantity ≠ quality
```

Evidence may justify exercising existing Authority. It does not manufacture new Authority.

Conflicting, incomplete, or weak Evidence should preserve appropriate uncertainty rather than manufacture certainty.

## Recursive Self-Evolution

URACE itself can participate in the lifecycle.

You do not need to notice every limitation and manually request every improvement.

```text
            GOVERNING INTENT
                   │
                   ▼
                 URACE
                   │
                 assess
                   │
         ┌─────────┴─────────┐
         ▼                   ▼
     Product gap          URACE gap
         │                   │
         ▼                   ▼

         │                   │
         └─────────┬─────────┘
                   ▼
               prioritize
                   │
                   ▼
                  plan
                   │
                   ▼
                 execute
                   │
                   ▼
                validate
                   │
                   ▼
               checkpoint
                   │
                   └──────────► reassess
```

Where existing Authority permits it, URACE may autonomously discover that its own implementation is limiting the governing Intent, promote that limitation into an Objective, prioritize it against Product work, plan the improvement, execute it through replaceable Executors, validate it, checkpoint the accepted change, and continue operating.

### Self-Evolution Is Not Self-Authorization

Autonomous initiation does not create Authority.

```text
discover justified
self-evolution
       │
       ▼
resolve Authority
       │
   ┌───┴───┐
   ▼       ▼
AUTHORIZED NOT AUTHORIZED
   │       │
   ▼       ▼
evolve   preserve boundary
```

URACE may not use self-evolution to create its own Authority, broaden delegation, override retained Intent, remove protected constraints, or bypass validation.

> **URACE may autonomously initiate its own evolution. It may not autonomously expand the Authority under which that evolution occurs.**

## Executors and Orchestrators

URACE depends on **roles and capabilities**, not particular products.

### Executor

An **Executor** is a replaceable system through which URACE delegates a bounded Operation requiring intelligence and/or execution.

An Executor may be an AI, agent, tool, API, script, deterministic system, orchestrator-mediated capability, human-approved mechanism, or another suitable system.

A **capable Executor** is capable for a particular Operation when it can accept the required bounded context, perform that Operation under applicable constraints, and return enough observable information for URACE to evaluate and validate the result.

A capable AI is therefore one possible capable Executor.

Capability is contextual. An Executor capable of bootstrapping URACE is not necessarily capable of every future Operation.

### Orchestrator

An **Orchestrator** is an optional replaceable execution-layer system that coordinates one or more Executors.

It may provide routing, sessions, continuation, status and result retrieval, failure handling, cancellation, context coordination, or similar execution facilities.

```text
                 URACE
                   │
            owns lifecycle
                   │
                   ▼
        optional ORCHESTRATOR
                   │
          coordinates execution
                   │
          ┌────────┼────────┐
          ▼        ▼        ▼
      EXECUTOR  EXECUTOR  EXECUTOR
```

A capable Orchestrator provides sufficient execution control and observability for URACE to delegate and supervise required bounded Operations while preserving URACE's lifecycle ownership and Executor replaceability.

> **Executors provide capabilities. Orchestrators coordinate execution. URACE owns the Product lifecycle.**

## Minimum and Recommended Execution Setups

URACE deliberately distinguishes **architectural sufficiency** from the **recommended richer general-purpose deployment**.

### Minimum

One capable Executor is sufficient when it can perform the Operations required by the applicable lifecycle.

```text
URACE
  │
  ▼
1 capable Executor
```

This is a valid URACE topology.

### Recommended General-Purpose Setup

For stronger general-purpose autonomous operation, the recommended topology is:

> **One capable Orchestrator plus at least three complementary capable AI Executors.**

```text
                         URACE
                           │
                  owns Product lifecycle
                           │
                           ▼
                 CAPABLE ORCHESTRATOR
                    e.g. OpenHands
                           │
              coordinates bounded execution
                           │
              ┌────────────┼────────────┐
              ▼            ▼            ▼
         CAPABLE AI    CAPABLE AI    CAPABLE AI
          Executor      Executor      Executor
```

A practical starting combination is:

```text
OpenHands
   │
   ├── Codex
   ├── Claude Code
   └── Gemini CLI
```

The recommendation is intentional: multiple complementary capable AIs give URACE a broader set of replaceable capabilities, specializations, alternative execution paths, and independent systems from which it can select according to the Operation.

The three AIs are not intended to participate in every Operation or occupy permanent universal roles.

URACE selects the appropriate available capability for the work at hand.

OpenHands, Codex, Claude Code, and Gemini CLI are example systems for this topology rather than architectural dependencies. Their current capabilities, availability, terms, and suitability should be verified for the intended deployment.

The distinction remains:

```text
MINIMUM

Idea / Intent
     +
1 capable Executor
     │
     ▼
   URACE
     │
     ▼
  Product


RECOMMENDED GENERAL-PURPOSE SETUP

Idea / Intent
     +
Capable Orchestrator
     +
3+ complementary
capable AI Executors
     │
     ▼
   URACE
     │
     ▼
persistent autonomous
Product evolution
```

> **One capable Executor is sufficient. A capable Orchestrator plus at least three complementary capable AI Executors is the recommended richer general-purpose setup.**

More Executors do not automatically make a deployment better. The recommendation is for complementary useful capabilities, not arbitrary Executor count.

Lifecycle continuity belongs to URACE rather than to a particular intelligence provider or conversation.

Replaceability does not imply equivalent capability, privacy, security, reliability, specialization, context capacity, cost, latency, or data handling.

```text
- prioritization
- planning and replanning
- Executor selection and delegation
- observation
- independent validation
- checkpointing
- recovery
```

A valid implementation should demonstrate, where applicable:

- operation with a single capable Executor;
- richer Orchestrator + multi-Executor operation where configured;
- replacement of Executors without losing lifecycle continuity;
- durable continuity across sessions and interruptions;
- Intent and retained-destination preservation;
- retained and delegated Authority;
- autonomous Objective discovery and prioritization;
- planning and replanning;
- autonomous Executor selection;
- Evidence and Trigger handling;
- course correction;
- independent validation;
- checkpointing and recovery;
- uncertain-effect reconciliation;
- dormancy and wakeability;
- multi-cycle Product evolution;
- autonomous discovery of justified self-evolution;
- authorized self-evolution without self-authorization;
- and replacement of richer execution infrastructure without transferring lifecycle ownership.

Repeated AI calls alone do not establish URACE.

A system that requires you to continually choose Objectives, Plans, routes, Executors, ordinary course corrections, or already-authorized self-improvements does not satisfy the intended lifecycle boundary.

> **Before bootstrap, capable execution systems create URACE. After bootstrap, URACE becomes the persistent lifecycle owner and uses those systems as replaceable capabilities beneath it.**

# Executor Data, Privacy, and Disclosure

> **Disclosure / Disclaimer:** URACE itself does not guarantee the confidentiality, retention behavior, security, **training or model-improvement use**, or other data practices of third-party Executors, Orchestrators, models, APIs, or services.

Executor replaceability does not make available systems equivalent in trust, privacy, security, or data handling.

When URACE delegates an Operation to an external system, supplied information may be transmitted, retained, logged, reviewed, used for model training or improvement, or otherwise processed according to the provider, product, account type, configuration, deployment model, contractual terms, retention settings, organizational controls, and current policies.

Whether a particular service uses submitted information for training or model improvement varies.

**Verify the current practices and applicable terms of each external system rather than assuming that all Executors or AI services handle data identically.**

## Why Executor Selection Matters

An Executor can be technically capable while still being unsuitable for the information required by an Operation.

```text
        OPERATION
            │
            ▼
   required information
            │
            ▼
          URACE
            │
            ▼
 "Which capable Executor
  may receive this data?"
            │
      ┌─────┴─────┐
      ▼           ▼
 suitable      unsuitable
      │           │
      ▼           ▼
 minimize      select another
 context        capability
      │
      ▼
   delegate
```

> **Capability does not imply disclosure Authority or suitability.**

Executor selection should therefore consider both what a system can do and what it may appropriately receive.

## Choosing an Executor

Depending on the Operation and deployment, relevant selection factors include:

- **Capability** — can it perform the required Operation?
- **Data sensitivity** — what proprietary, personal, customer, regulated, or confidential information would it receive?
- **Disclosure Authority** — may that information legitimately be disclosed to it?
- **Training and model improvement** — may supplied information be used to train or improve models or services?
- **Retention and logging** — what may be retained or logged, and for how long?
- **Security and isolation** — what protections and deployment boundaries apply?
- **Privacy and compliance** — does the deployment satisfy applicable requirements and policies?
- **Context minimization** — can the Operation be completed with less information?
- **Reliability and observability** — can URACE sufficiently observe and validate the result?
- **Specialization** — is another Executor better suited to the Operation?
- **Cost, latency, and availability** — is it operationally appropriate?

For sensitive or proprietary information, appropriate controls may include context minimization, deterministic tools, local or self-hosted Executors, isolated infrastructure, private deployments, or enterprise/API offerings with suitable terms.

Those are options, not guarantees.

Do not assume that changing a model, Executor, Orchestrator, API, account tier, or deployment mode automatically provides a particular confidentiality, retention, security, or training policy.

**Verify the actual service, configuration, and applicable terms.**

> **Choose an Executor for both what it can do and what it may safely and legitimately receive.**

This applies equally to the recommended multi-AI setup. More available intelligence does not justify sending information to an Executor that is unsuitable or unauthorized to receive it.
